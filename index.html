How the service works

Three steps, no workflow change for recipients

Recipients open ordinary image files. There's no viewer, plug-in or DRM, so there's nothing for them to notice or work around.

Send us the images and recipients

Share the assets and the list of people or organisations receiving them. Each recipient gets an opaque ID, so no personal data goes into the image.

Every recipient gets their own copy

Each copy looks identical but carries a unique, signed mark. The signing key stays on our embedding host, so nobody can forge a mark that frames someone else.

Found a leak? Send it to us

We read the mark from the leaked file alone and return a report naming the recipient, with a signature that proves the mark was ours.

Detection stays private, deliberately. A public "check this image" tool would let an attacker probe it until the mark is gone. We run every trace ourselves, which is part of what keeps your marks durable.
Measured, not promised

What a leaked copy can survive

Leaked images rarely arrive intact. These results are the worst case across four very different image types. In every test run, a failed trace came back as "not found", never as the wrong recipient. Missing a leaker can be recovered from. Accusing the wrong person can't.

Signed, not guessed

A match is confirmed by an Ed25519 signature, not a checksum. When we say "found", we mean found.

Bound to each image

A mark lifted from one image and pasted into another won't verify, so it can't be moved to frame someone.

Resists collusion

For high-stakes releases, anti-collusion fingerprinting still points at recipients who compare and blend their copies.
